The dish people hover over at the holiday table, waiting for the first spoon to break through the crust. Three cheeses melted into a sauce of evaporated milk and cream, layered with more cheese and baked until the top is golden and crackling and the middle pulls into long, stretchy strings.

Grating your own cheese is the one job everyone wants to skip, and it's the one that matters most: it's the difference between a sauce that's silky and one that's grainy. Take it slowly, whisking in the milk and cream a little at a time, and the reward is a dish people talk about long after the plates are cleared.

## Ingredients

- 450 g (1 lb) cavatappi (corkscrew pasta)
- 450 g (1 lb) mozzarella, from a block
- 450 g (1 lb) Colby cheese, from a block
- 225 g (8 oz) vintage cheddar
- 1 tsp salt, or to taste
- 1 tsp pepper, or to taste
- 1 tsp garlic powder, or to taste
- 1 tsp smoked paprika, or to taste
- 45 g (3 tbsp) butter
- 24 g (3 tbsp) plain flour
- 1 can (about 350 ml / 12 oz) evaporated milk (not condensed milk)
- 480 ml (2 cups) thickened cream
- 1 good spoonful Dijon mustard
- A splash of milk, if the sauce is too thick
- Sliced spring onions, chives or parsley, to garnish

## Method

1. Preheat the oven to 180°C (160°C fan / 350°F / gas 4).
2. Bring a large pot of water to the boil over high heat and salt it well. Once it's at a rolling boil, add the pasta and boil for 8 minutes, stirring every 2 minutes. Drain well.
3. While the water comes to the boil and the pasta cooks, grate all the cheese yourself on the coarse side of a box grater (pre-grated cheese has a coating that turns the sauce grainy and stops it melting properly). Mix the three cheeses together in a large bowl and divide them into two even portions.
4. Mix the salt, pepper, garlic powder and smoked paprika together in a small bowl. The amounts are a starting point: taste the sauce as you go and add more of anything you like.
5. Melt the butter in a large pot over medium heat. Add half the seasoning and stir for about 30 seconds to bloom it.
6. Add the flour and cook for about 2 minutes, stirring constantly, until it bubbles slightly and looks combined, so the flour cooks out and the sauce doesn't turn grainy.
7. Switch to a whisk. Add the evaporated milk a little at a time, whisking out any lumps before adding more. Pouring it in all at once makes the sauce soupy.
8. Add the cream the same way, pouring and whisking a little at a time. This takes about 5–7 minutes.
9. Whisk in the Dijon mustard and the rest of the seasoning. Let the sauce come to a simmer and thicken, whisking now and then.
10. Turn the heat to low, or off, so the cheese doesn't curdle and split. Add half the cheese a handful at a time, whisking until each handful has melted before adding the next. If the sauce becomes too thick to stir, thin it with a splash of milk.
11. Switch back to a spatula and stir in the pasta until every piece is coated in sauce. Taste a piece of pasta and adjust the seasoning.
12. Spread half the pasta evenly in a 23 × 33 cm (9 × 13 inch) baking dish and scatter over half of the remaining cheese. Add the rest of the pasta, then finish with an even layer of the last of the cheese.
13. Bake for 25–30 minutes, until the top melts and bubbles. Grill for about 2 minutes for a golden crust.
14. Let it sit for a few minutes so it's not too hot to eat, then garnish with spring onions, chives or parsley and serve.

## Notes

- Colby is in the supermarket cheese fridge next to the cheddar.
- To make ahead, assemble it up to 3 days before, cover tightly and keep it in the fridge. Bake covered with foil for about 30 minutes, then uncover and bake until heated through before grilling the top.
