The dish people hover over at the holiday table, waiting for the first spoon to break through the crust. Three cheeses melted into a sauce of evaporated milk and cream, layered with more cheese and baked until the top is golden and crackling and the middle pulls into long, stretchy strings.

Grating your own cheese is the one job everyone wants to skip, and it's the one that matters most: it's the difference between a sauce that's silky and one that's grainy. Take it slowly, whisking in the milk and cream a little at a time, and the reward is a dish people talk about long after the plates are cleared.

## Ingredients

- 450 g (1 lb) cavatappi (corkscrew pasta)
- 450 g (1 lb) mozzarella
- 450 g (1 lb) Colby cheese
- 225 g (8 oz) cheddar
- 1 tsp salt, or to taste
- 1 tsp pepper, or to taste
- 1 tsp garlic powder, or to taste
- 1 tsp smoked paprika, or to taste
- 45 g (3 tbsp) butter
- 24 g (3 tbsp) plain (all-purpose) flour
- 1 can (about 350 ml / 12 oz) evaporated milk (not condensed milk)
- 480 ml (2 cups) thickened (heavy) cream
- 1 good spoonful Dijon mustard
- A splash of milk, if the sauce is too thick
- Sliced spring onions, chives or parsley, to garnish

## Method

1. Bring a large pot of water to the boil and salt it well. Add the pasta and boil for 8 minutes, stirring every 2 minutes. Drain.
2. Grate all the cheese yourself (pre-grated cheese has a coating that turns the sauce grainy and stops it melting properly). Mix the three cheeses together and divide them in half.
3. Mix the salt, pepper, garlic powder and smoked paprika together. The amounts are a starting point: taste the sauce as you go and add more of anything you like.
4. Melt the butter in a large pot over medium heat. Add half the seasoning and cook for about 30 seconds to bloom it.
5. Add the flour and stir until combined. Cook for about 2 minutes, still over medium heat, so the flour cooks out and the sauce doesn't turn grainy.
6. Switch to a whisk. Add the evaporated milk a little at a time, whisking out any lumps before adding more. Pouring it in all at once makes the sauce soupy.
7. Add the cream the same way, pouring and whisking a little at a time. This takes about 5–7 minutes.
8. Whisk in the rest of the seasoning and the Dijon mustard.
9. Turn the heat to low, or off, so the cheese doesn't curdle and split. Add half the cheese a handful at a time, whisking until each handful has melted before adding the next. If the sauce becomes too thick to stir, thin it with a splash of milk.
10. Switch back to a spatula and fold the pasta through the cheese sauce. Taste a piece of pasta and adjust the seasoning.
11. Preheat the oven to 180°C (160°C fan / 350°F / gas 4).
12. Spread half the pasta in a baking dish and scatter over half of the remaining cheese. Add the rest of the pasta, then the last of the cheese on top.
13. Bake for 25–30 minutes, then grill for a few minutes until the top has a golden crust.
14. Garnish with spring onions, chives or parsley.
