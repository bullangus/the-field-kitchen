The cinnamon and cardamom go into the hot oil with the onions, the curry leaves crackle, and the whole kitchen smells like Durban. Bunny chow is South Africa's great street food: a chicken, potato and chickpea curry ladled into a hollowed-out loaf of bread. It's on the table in under an hour.

The fun is in the serving. Everyone gets their own little cob, filled to the brim with the lid set back on top, and the bread soaks up the curry as you go. A spoonful of Onion & Tomato Relish on the side keeps it fresh. When there are no cobs to be had, it's just as good in a bowl with bread for dipping.

## Ingredients

- 120 ml (½ cup) canola or vegetable oil
- 1 medium onion, diced
- 2 tsp minced garlic
- 1 tsp minced ginger
- 1 cinnamon stick
- 1 tbsp curry powder, or more to taste
- 2–3 curry leaves, chopped
- 3 green cardamom pods
- 1½ tsp paprika
- ½ tsp cayenne pepper
- 2 medium tomatoes, diced
- 450 g (1 lb) skinless boneless chicken thighs, cut into bite-sized pieces
- 225 g (8 oz) potatoes or a little less, scrubbed and cut into cubes
- 400 g tin chickpeas, rinsed and drained
- 360 ml (1½ cups) chicken stock or water, or more as needed
- Salt and pepper
- 4 small cob loaves

## Method

1. Heat the oil in a large saucepan. Add the onion, garlic, ginger, cinnamon stick, curry powder, curry leaves, cardamom pods, paprika and cayenne pepper. Cook for 2–3 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the onion is translucent.
2. Add the tomatoes, then the chicken. Stir and sauté for 2–3 minutes more, adding a splash of the chicken stock or water if anything starts to catch.
3. Add the potatoes, chickpeas and chicken stock, adding more as needed. Bring to the boil, then simmer until the sauce thickens, about 25 minutes or more.
4. Season with salt and pepper, and add a little more stock or water if the curry is too thick.
5. Cut a lid off the top of each cob loaf and pull out the soft bread inside, leaving a thick wall. Ladle in the curry, set the lid back on top and serve.

## Notes

- Instead of the cob loaves, serve the curry in bowls with bread on the side for dipping.
