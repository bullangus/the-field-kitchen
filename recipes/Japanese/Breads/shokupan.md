Japanese milk bread: soft, pillowy and faintly sweet. Baked in a lidded 1.5 kin shokupan loaf pan (20 × 12 × 12.5 cm) for a flat top, or without the lid for a rounded one. Use it for the Egg Sando.

## Ingredients

- 275 g water (see step 1 for its temperature)
- 22 g sugar
- 11 g honey
- 8 g instant yeast
- 385 g bread flour, plus about 2 tsp for dusting
- 8 g table salt
- 22 g skim milk powder
- 28 g unsalted butter, cut into small cubes
- ½ tsp neutral oil, for greasing the bowl
- 10 g unsalted butter, at room temperature, for greasing the loaf pan

## Method

### Dough

1. Weigh everything on digital scales. Pick the water temperature to suit the kitchen, because fast kneading heats the dough: room-temperature water (20–24°C) in a very cold kitchen, 18–20°C in a cool one, 16–18°C in a moderate one, and 12–16°C in a warm or hot one. The aim is a dough at 26–28°C (79–82°F) after kneading; adjust the water by a few degrees next time if it isn't.
2. In a large, straight-sided glass bowl, combine the water, sugar and honey. Whisk in the yeast and set aside.
3. Put the flour, salt and milk powder in the bowl of a stand mixer and mix well. Make a well in the centre and pour in the yeast mixture, scraping out every drop. Keep the glass bowl for proofing. Fit the dough hook.

### Knead

1. Knead on speed 2 for 2–3 minutes, until a shaggy dough forms with no dry patches.
2. Increase to speed 4 and knead for 4 minutes. The dough will be smoother but still a little rough and bumpy.
3. Increase to speed 6 and knead for 3 minutes, or until the dough clears the sides of the bowl. Hold the mixer steady with both hands; it will shake and can walk across the bench.
4. Check the dough is below 26°C (79°F), then add the cubed butter. Use cold butter in summer and room-temperature butter in winter. Knead on speed 2 for 2 minutes, until no streaks of butter remain.
5. Increase to speed 6 and knead for 2–3 minutes, holding the mixer. Stop at 2 minutes and lift the hook: once the dough comes together in one piece and clings to the hook, leaving the bowl clean, do the windowpane test straight away. If it doesn't cling, keep kneading and check again at 3 minutes, then every 30–60 seconds. Don't let the dough go above 28°C (82°F), or the gluten collapses into a slack, gooey mess that can't be saved.
6. Windowpane test: tear off a small piece of dough and gently stretch it into a square. It passes if it forms a thin, translucent membrane without tearing. If it tears, knead on speed 6 for 1 more minute and test again.
7. Knead on speed 4 for 1–2 minutes to relax the dough and smooth its surface (or just a few seconds if you're worried about over-kneading). The dough should gather in one piece and feel soft, smooth and silky. Check its temperature in the centre: it should be 26–28°C (79–82°F). If it's warmer, rest it in the fridge for 15 minutes.

### First rise

1. Lightly dust the bench with flour. Thinly coat the glass bowl with the oil and wipe off any excess.
2. Scrape the dough onto the bench. Fold it over itself so the smooth side faces up, and tuck the edges underneath to form a smooth oval. Keep the smooth side up from here on.
3. Hold the dough by its far edge and slam the smooth side firmly onto the bench. Fold the bottom edge up and away from you, tuck the sides underneath and scoop it up with both hands. Repeat 5 times in total, until the dough is a taut, smooth ball.
4. Cup both hands around the dough and gently drag it towards you, tucking the bottom under to tighten the surface. Turn it slightly and repeat until it's perfectly smooth and taut.
5. Put the dough in the oiled bowl, cover with plastic wrap and leave in a warm spot until doubled in size, 45–60 minutes (up to 1½ hours in a cool kitchen). If it looks flat halfway through, gently reshape it into a tall dome without knocking the air out.
6. Finger test: dust a little flour on the dough and poke the centre with your index finger. It's ready if the dent stays or springs back very slowly. If it springs back straight away, let it rise longer and test again.

### Shape

1. Turn the bowl upside down to release the dough onto the bench, smooth side down. Gently press it with your fingers to deflate it.
2. Gather the edges into the centre and weigh the dough, then divide it into 3 equal pieces with a dough scraper. To even them up, pinch a bit from the edge of a large piece and press it onto the cut edge of a smaller one, keeping the smooth surface intact.
3. Hold a piece smooth side up and gently pull the dough down on two opposite sides to round it. Turn it 90 degrees and repeat. Put it on an unfloured part of the bench, drag it 5 cm (2 inches) towards you and turn it a quarter turn; do this 6 times, until smooth and taut. Repeat with the other pieces, giving each the same tension.
4. Cover the balls with a well-wrung damp tea towel and rest for 15 minutes.
5. Grease the loaf pan and its lid with the 10 g butter in a thin, even coat, taking care in the corners.
6. Take one ball, keeping the others covered. Lightly flour the bench and the top of the dough. Press the rolling pin into the centre, then roll from the centre to the top edge and from the centre to the bottom edge to push out the gas. Flatten any thick edges and air bubbles with the heel of your hand. If the dough springs back, cover it and rest for 2–3 minutes rather than forcing it.
7. Turn the dough 90 degrees and flip it over. Gently lift the edges to let it relax. Roll from the centre, away from you and back, to an even thickness, squaring off the corners to make an 18 × 23 cm (7 × 9 inch) rectangle with a short side towards you.
8. Fold the dough in thirds lengthways, like a letter: bring one side to the centre and overlap it with the other. Press the edges to seal. Roll over it with the pin, away and back, to press out any trapped air.
9. Fold the two top corners down to meet in the centre, making a point. Starting from the point, roll the dough slowly towards you, pulling gently so the surface is smooth and taut but not too tight. Pinch the seam to seal and put the log seam side down under the damp towel. Repeat with the other pieces, giving each the same tension.
10. Put the first and second logs seam side down at opposite ends of the loaf pan, with their swirls facing inwards and their sides touching the walls. Put the last log between them, seam side down. Gently press the tops so they're level.

### Final rise

1. Cover the pan with plastic wrap and leave in a warm place for about 1 hour. For a flat top, the dough should reach 75–80% of the pan's height, which leaves room for it to spring in the oven. For a rounded top, it should reach 85–90%, or just touch the plastic wrap. If proofing in the oven, take the pan out 20–30 minutes early so the oven can preheat.

### Bake

1. Preheat the oven to 220°C (205°C fan / 425°F / gas 7), with the rack placed so the top of the pan sits 15–18 cm (6–7 inches) below the top element.
2. Remove the plastic wrap. For a flat top, close the lid, lower the oven to 210°C (195°C fan / 410°F) and bake for 25–30 minutes. If the dough has risen past 80%, bake it as a rounded loaf instead, or the lid may stick. For a rounded top, spritz the surface with water, lower the oven to 195°C (180°C fan / 385°F) and bake for 25–30 minutes.
3. As soon as it comes out of the oven, slam the pan firmly on the bench once or twice to release the trapped steam, so the sides don't cave in. Work quickly from here so the base doesn't go soggy. Open the lid; if it's stuck, slam the pan again, or return it to the oven for 2–3 minutes.
4. Turn the pan on its side over a wire rack and shake firmly until the loaf slides out. Cool completely, 2–3 hours, before slicing with a bread knife. Cutting it hot lets the moisture escape and dries out the bread.

## Notes

- Active dry yeast works too: use 25% more (10 g), stir it into warm water (38–43°C) and leave for 10 minutes until foamy before using.
- A warm place is 30–35°C. A turned-off oven with a bowl of boiling water inside, or a microwave with a cup of just-boiled water, both work.
- The kneading times suit a 5-quart KitchenAid Artisan. With other mixers, follow the steps until the dough passes the windowpane test.
- Don't wash the loaf pan: wipe it with dry paper towel. If you must rinse it, dry it straight away and season it again.
- Keeps in a plastic bag in a cool place for 1–2 days; don't refrigerate, which dries it out. For longer, slice and freeze for up to a month.
