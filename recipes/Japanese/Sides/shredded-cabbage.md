The mound of fine, feathery cabbage that comes beside almost anything fried in Japan. A quarter of a drum cabbage, shaved on a mandoline, lightly salted and squeezed.

It's the quiet side that makes a plate of tonkatsu feel complete: cool, crisp and clean against the hot, crunchy crumb, and the perfect thing to drag through the last of the tonkatsu sauce. A mandoline makes it effortless and gets the shreds far finer than a knife.

## Ingredients

- ¼ drum cabbage
- ½ tsp salt

## Method

1. Cut the hard core out of the quarter with a V-shaped cut.
2. Shave the cabbage on a mandoline into fine shreds, using the guard to push it through. Run the cut face across the leaf veins for soft, fluffy shreds, or along them for crisper ones. When the piece gets too small to hold safely, finely shred the rest with a knife.
3. Rinse the shreds under running water, then soak them in iced water for 2–3 minutes, no longer, or they lose their flavour.
4. Drain well and spin dry in a salad spinner.
5. Toss the cabbage with the salt and leave it for 10 minutes to soften slightly.
6. Squeeze out the excess water with your hands, then fluff the shreds apart and pile them beside the Tonkatsu.

## Notes

- Keeps in the fridge for up to 3 days in a container lined with a damp, well-wrung paper towel, with another on top.
