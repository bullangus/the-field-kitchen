Double-fried wings glazed in a sticky, sweet soy, mirin and ginger sauce and finished with sesame seeds. The sauce keeps in the fridge for a month.

## Ingredients

- 450 g (1 lb) chicken wings, flats and drumettes (this much sauce covers up to 900 g / 2 lb)
- 35 g (¼ cup) potato starch or cornflour (cornstarch)
- 700 ml (3 cups) neutral oil, for deep-frying
- Toasted white sesame seeds

### For the sauce

- 240 ml (1 cup) mirin
- 120 ml (½ cup) soy sauce
- 45 ml (3 tbsp) sake
- 65 g (⅓ cup) sugar
- ⅛ tsp fine salt
- 1 knob fresh ginger (about 1.3 cm / ½ inch), sliced
- 2 cloves garlic, crushed or minced
- ½ tsp MSG

## Method

1. For the sauce, put the mirin, soy sauce, sake, sugar, salt, ginger, garlic and MSG in a small saucepan and bring to the boil. Reduce the heat and simmer for 15–20 minutes, until reduced and thickened. It thickens more as it cools.
2. Meanwhile, cut the wings at the joint if needed and pat dry with paper towel.
3. Dredge the wings in the potato starch and shake off the excess.
4. Heat the oil in a heavy-based pot over medium heat to 160°C (320°F). Fry the wings until about 80% cooked, 8–10 minutes. Drain on a wire rack or paper towel for 3–5 minutes.
5. Raise the oil to 180°C (350°F) and fry the wings a second time until golden brown and crisp, 3–5 minutes.
6. Toss the wings in the sauce with tongs, or plate them and pour the sauce over. Sprinkle with sesame seeds and serve straight away.

## Notes

- Leftover sauce keeps in a jar in the fridge for up to a month.
- Leftover wings keep in an airtight container in the fridge for 3–4 days, or in the freezer for a month.
- The original uses ¼ tsp Diamond Crystal kosher salt, which isn't sold in Australia; ⅛ tsp fine salt is the same amount of salt.
- 2026-10-28: Added ½ tsp MSG to the sauce. We add it to most savoury dishes.
