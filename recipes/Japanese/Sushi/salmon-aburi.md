The hiss of the blowtorch, the Kewpie blistering and browning, and that smoky, toasty smell. Salmon nigiri piped with Kewpie, seared until golden, then glossed with a sweet soy and mirin tare. You'll need a kitchen blowtorch, and sashimi-grade salmon bought on the day.

The torch is the fun part. The top of the salmon just starts to cook while the middle stays silky, and the Kewpie turns golden and smoky. A last drizzle of tare brings sweetness and shine. They're best eaten straight away, while the tops are still warm.

## Ingredients

### For the tare

- 60 ml (¼ cup) soy sauce
- 60 ml (¼ cup) mirin
- 2 tbsp sugar

### For the rice

- 300 g (2 rice-cooker cups / 360 ml) sushi rice
- 60 ml (¼ cup) rice vinegar
- 15 ml (1 tbsp) mirin
- 1 tbsp sugar
- 1 tsp salt

### To assemble

- 200 g sashimi-grade salmon fillet, skin and bones removed
- Kewpie mayonnaise

## Method

### Tare

1. Put the soy sauce, mirin and sugar in a small saucepan and stir over medium heat until the sugar dissolves.
2. Simmer for about 5 minutes, until syrupy. Let it cool; it thickens as it cools.

### Nigiri

1. Rinse the rice until the water runs almost clear, then cook it in a rice cooker or pot.
2. While the rice cooks, stir the rice vinegar, mirin, sugar and salt together until the sugar and salt dissolve. While the rice is hot, tip it into a wide bowl and fold the seasoning through with a rice paddle, using a cutting motion so the grains don't get mashed. Let it cool to room temperature.
3. With a sharp knife, slice the salmon across the grain into about 12 slices, about 5 mm thick.
4. With wet hands, shape the rice into about 12 firm oblong mounds. Lay a slice of salmon over each and press gently so it hugs the rice.
5. Pipe a zigzag of Kewpie mayonnaise over each piece.
6. Sear the tops with a kitchen blowtorch until the Kewpie blisters and browns and the salmon just starts to cook.
7. Drizzle with the tare and serve straight away.
