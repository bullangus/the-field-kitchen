Soft, glossy slices of salmon draped over little mounds of seasoned rice, with a hidden dab of wasabi in between. The simplest sushi there is, and the one that shows off good fish. Buy sashimi-grade salmon on the day you make it.

The joy is in its simplicity: a firm press of rice, a clean slice of fish, and nothing to hide behind. Dip the salmon side lightly in soy sauce, never the rice, and clear the palate with pickled ginger between pieces. It sits beautifully on a platter beside Salmon Aburi and Chicken Avocado Nigiri.

## Ingredients

### For the rice

- 300 g (2 rice-cooker cups / 360 ml) sushi rice
- 60 ml (¼ cup) rice vinegar
- 15 ml (1 tbsp) mirin
- 1 tbsp sugar
- 1 tsp salt

### To assemble

- 200 g sashimi-grade salmon fillet, skin and bones removed
- Wasabi
- Soy sauce, to serve
- Pickled ginger, to serve

## Method

1. Rinse the rice until the water runs almost clear, then cook it in a rice cooker or pot.
2. While the rice cooks, stir the rice vinegar, mirin, sugar and salt together until the sugar and salt dissolve. While the rice is hot, tip it into a wide bowl and fold the seasoning through with a rice paddle, using a cutting motion so the grains don't get mashed. Let it cool to room temperature.
3. With a sharp knife, slice the salmon across the grain into about 12 slices, about 5 mm thick.
4. With wet hands, shape the rice into about 12 firm oblong mounds.
5. Dab a little wasabi on the underside of each slice of salmon, lay it over a mound of rice and press gently so it hugs the rice.
6. Serve with soy sauce for dipping and pickled ginger on the side.
